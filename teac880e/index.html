Instructional Technology in Mathematics

University of Nebraska–Lincoln | 3 credit hours | Artifacts online

What this course was

Technology in the mathematics classroom, taken the same term as the qualitative methods course and sharing its major deliverable with it. The proposal I wrote for EDPS 900K in April was submitted here in May with a different title page — a YPAR study of mathematics and technology in a secondary classroom, framed through critical race theory, with a target community rather than a site. It is published once, below, and linked from both pages.

The course’s own artifact is the Technology Statement, required as a draft in Module 3 and again as a final in Module 7. It is a position paper: what I think technology is for in a mathematics classroom and what it is not for. Both versions open with the same three epigraphs — Kovalevskaya, Einstein and Weierstrass, each insisting that a mathematician who is not also a poet is incomplete.

Dissertation corpus

Through line

Those three epigraphs also open the TEAC 949B final paper from the previous autumn — a different course, a different assignment, the same frame. I did not plan the repetition and did not notice it until both documents were laid out for this portfolio. Two years running, whatever I was about to argue about mathematics, I began by insisting it was a kind of poetry.

The draft and the final of the Technology Statement are 545 and 543 words. The stance did not move across the course. That is either a sign that the position was already settled, or a sign that the course did not manage to unsettle it. I am not certain which, and the two documents cannot tell me.

The proposal’s Researcher’s Statement announces itself as “adapted from my ‘Personal Curriculum Platform’ essay from EDU 601 Critical Issues at Doane University” — a master’s essay from 2020 reused as the positionality statement of a 2024 doctoral proposal, with the citation trail left visible. Reusing one’s own earlier writing as a source, and saying so, is a small version of the dissertation’s method.
