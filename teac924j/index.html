TEAC 924J

Equity and STEM Education Research

University of Nebraska–Lincoln | 3 credit hours | Artifacts online

What this course was

Fifteen weeks on equity in STEM education research, which in practice meant reading a great many reports that counted people and declined to ask why the counting came out the way it did. The National Center for Science and Engineering Statistics report we opened with was the clearest case. The field had diversified over a decade, it said, and remained dominated by white and Asian men. It reported gender, or race, or disability — one axis at a time, never together, and never in a way that would have required it to explain anything.

The output was a Youth Participatory Action Research proposal: a study of mathematics education and technology access in a secondary mathematics classroom, built on the premise that Western mathematics presents itself as immutable, objective and True, and that this is dehumanizing to students required to learn something which denies their own knowledge and experience. It never became a study.

What survived it was the equity stance statement, which the course made me write twice — once at the start and once at the end. The two documents are not the same. Three years on, the dissertation is not YPAR and is not about anyone else's classroom. The reading list from this term is still the reading list.

Dissertation corpus

Through line

The course required the equity stance statement to be written twice, once at the start of the term and once at the end. Neither was written as data. Both are data now: a pre- and post-measure of a single practitioner’s stance, taken three years before there was a study to attach it to, by an instrument that was not trying to measure anything.

The proposal itself never became a study, and the dissertation is not YPAR and is not about anyone else’s classroom. The reading list from this term is still the reading list.
