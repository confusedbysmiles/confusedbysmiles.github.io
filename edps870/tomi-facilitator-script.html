ToMI on Trial — Facilitator Script

Private — keep this off the projector. Target 30–40 min: ~21 presenting, ~15 discussion.

Setup (before class)

  • Open tomi_on_trial.html in a browser and press F for fullscreen.

  • Split the room: left half = Defense (the scale is valid), right half = Prosecution (it over-reaches). The House Rules screen says this on-screen, so just gesture left and right.

  • Drive with Space (reveal / advance) and ← → (move). Award points with D (Defense) and P — the running tally is top-right.

  • Cold open, in character: ‘All rise. The People versus the ToMI scale. The instrument is accused of claiming to measure an identity — you are the jury.’

The game runs a Defense vs Prosecution scoreboard. Award with the D / P keys (or the −/+ buttons, top-right). Max ~13 points across the trial.

Round I — Voir Dire: name the evidence

Read each exhibit aloud. Class votes by hand for the evidence type; reveal the ruling; award the point (above). The ‘say this’ column is your terminology cue — graders reward precise psychometric language.

Ex.Name itSay this
AInternal consistency (reliability)Cronbach’s α = how tightly items in a subscale agree. Reliability, not validity. Flag: .950 is high enough to suspect redundant items.
BInternal structure — EFAItems group onto the 3 hypothesized factors. KMO .915 = sampling adequacy; oblimin = oblique rotation, correct since subscales should correlate.
CInternal structure — CFA (+ replication)Confirms the EFA model on a fresh sample. χ² significant but N-sensitive; RMSEA .060 / CFI .963 / SRMR .049 all good.
DKnown-groups (relations to other vars)Groups theory says should differ (5+ vs 0–5 yrs) do. d up to 0.84 = large effect.
EConvergent (relations to other vars)Scores track linked variables (wellbeing, network) in the predicted direction. Note wellbeing edges toward a criterion.
FDiscriminant (relations to other vars)A predicted null: relates to math PL, not general PL → it’s a math identity, not generic teacher identity.

Round III — Closing Arguments: facilitate the debate

Charge One — is it identity, or three feelings about math? (Acquit → D · Convict → P)

  • Darragh’s own warning (quoted in the paper): identity gets diluted when reduced to affect. Did they dodge it, or fall in?

  • What evidence would separate ‘has a math-teacher identity’ from ‘likes teaching math and feels competent’?

  • Unified view: one construct validity, many evidence sources. Does ‘three correlated factors’ demonstrate identity?

Charge Two — does it need a retrial? (your launchpad) (Acquit → D · Retrial → P)

  • Sample is self-selected association members; enthusiasm is negatively skewed. Who’s missing — and how would it behave on pre-service teachers?

  • It’s a state measured cross-sectionally. Can a snapshot capture an identity? What would longitudinal data add?

  • Life history is absent — the authors point to Cribbs et al. (2020). That biographical → pedagogy gap is exactly the instrument you’re building. Land the talk here.

  • Next iteration: which subscale do you cut, keep, or add — and what evidence would you collect first?

If the room goes quiet (facilitation moves)

  • Silence → ‘Argue the prosecution’s side for a second — what’s the weakest link here?’

  • One-word answer → ‘Say more — what evidence would change your mind?’

  • One voice dominating → ‘Let’s hear from the other bench — someone who hasn’t weighed in.’

  • Drift → ‘Park that — bring it back on Charge Two.’

  • Build on a good point → name the student’s idea, then ask the room to push on it.

  • Always close a thread by naming the psychometric concept it surfaced — that’s what the depth score rewards.

Time checks: glance at the clock at the end of Round I (~14 min in) and Round II (~21 min in). If behind, Round I can drop to 5 exhibits (cut E and G) — never cut Round III, it’s 30 of your 100 points.
