Introduction to Educational and Psychological Measurement

University of Nebraska–Lincoln | 3 credit hours | Artifacts online

What this course was

The last course on the program of study, taken as a five-week summer class that appeared unexpectedly and let me finish coursework a semester early. Measurement: what it means to say an instrument measures a thing, what counts as evidence that it does, and how much of the vocabulary — reliability, validity, alpha — gets used to mean something it does not. Two deliverables. Build a measure. Put someone else’s on trial.

The measure I built is a thirty-item Math Teacher Identity Instrument for pre-service teachers, across three subscales: previous experiences as a learner of mathematics, perceptions of what mathematics is, and pedagogy and identity as a teacher of it. Four-point agreement, no neutral option — please lean one way on every item. The respondent-facing form withholds subscale membership and keying; the item banks give them.

The validity study was of the ToMI scale — Willis and colleagues’ 2023 teacher-of-mathematics identity measure — and I delivered it as The People v. The ToMI Scale: the room split into defense and prosecution, the paper’s validity evidence entered exhibit by exhibit, points awarded from the keyboard, and a facilitator script marked keep this off the projector. Alpha is a reliability coefficient, not a validity one. The cleverest move in the paper is a predicted null. Higher alpha is not automatically better. The jury was the class.

Dissertation corpus

Through line

The instrument I built measures, in other people, what the dissertation examines in me. Subscale 1 is previous experiences as learners — the biographical roots of a mathematics teacher’s identity, as items. I wrote it in July 2026, five months after the dissertation was reframed to excavate exactly that in a single participant, and I did not connect the two while writing it. The item bank for Subscale 3 defines its low pole as “the ‘banking’/transmission model, teacher as sole authority, one correct answer, passive students.” That is Freire’s second chapter, turned into a Likert anchor.

The scale I put on trial is a validated measure of the dissertation’s own construct. Teacher-of-mathematics identity, with published factor structure, known-groups evidence, and a discriminant null — encountered in the last course of the program, after the proposal was written and the methods chosen. The dissertation’s quantitative strand had settled on summary statistics over coded artifacts. This is the point at which an instrument existed that could have been administered instead, or as well. Whether to reopen a settled design for it is a question the comprehensive exams now ask directly, and I built this course’s artifacts before that question had been written.

Prosecution or defense, the trial format was the same move as the rest of the program: take a thing that presents itself as settled — a scale, a grade, a curriculum — and make the room argue about what it actually measures.
