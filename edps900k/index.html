EDPS 900K

Qualitative Approaches to Educational Research

University of Nebraska–Lincoln | 3 credit hours | Artifacts online

What this course was

My first formal qualitative methods course, with Wayne Babchuk. The sequence was the standard one and it did its job: a critique of a published qualitative study, a midterm that made me name a research paradigm in writing for the first time, and a full research proposal with a presentation to match. The critique took Husbye’s 2021 multiple case study of play in literacy teacher preparation and read it against its own methodological claims.

The proposal was Youth Participatory Action Research in a secondary mathematics classroom — a study of how mathematics education and technology intersect, framed through critical race theory, in which the students would be co-researchers and the specific questions would be theirs to set. It named a community rather than a site, because YPAR does not permit the researcher to arrive with the questions already written.

Dissertation corpus

Through line

This proposal is the study the dissertation did not become. It went forward: the IRB protocol built from it was approved in November 2024 after four rounds of revision, and it ran as the pilot documented in TEAC 995. Its final report was filed in February 2026 — two days after the purpose statement that reframed the dissertation around a single participant, me. The proposal and the reframe are both dated. The road not taken is documented at both ends.

The reason it was not taken is in the proposal itself. YPAR insists the questions belong to the participants. A study in which the researcher is the only participant is the limit case of that insistence, not a departure from it. I did not see that in April 2024.

The midterm’s first question asked for a paradigm — where a researcher stands among the five broad epistemological positions, and what that stance does to what they can claim. It was the first time I had been required to say. The answer is not reproduced here, because the question is still in use.
