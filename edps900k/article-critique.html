Research Article Critique

Article critique

Article Summary and Major Characteristics

Husbye’s (2021) article titled Leveraging the Power of Play in Rehearsals is a multiple case study qualitative research study using a practitioner inquiry lens looking at the impact of play within school-based literacy teacher preparation programs. Their decision to conduct this research was based on the expanding research available indicating the power of play in education, but the lack of use of play within the context of teacher education. The goal of this research was to contribute to this field specifically regarding the impact of play on pre-service literacy teacher education.

The research was conducted at a midsized urban university in the Midwest within their literacy education course with pre-service teachers enrolled in the university’s teacher education program. Data was collected over the course of five semesters (Spring 2016 – Spring 2018). The data collected included mid- and end-of-semester interviews, audio/visual recordings of the rehearsals done by students, video recordings of the enactments, and various other artifacts created by the pre-service teachers throughout the duration of the research.

Husbye’s (2021) research found that “play, utilized within the context of a literacy education course, promoted the development of complexity tolerance: an ability to entertain the variables that may impact their [pre-service teachers] teaching, even those they had not thought of” (p. 1). From these findings, Husbye recommends further research be conducted to explore this potential development of complexity tolerance in pre-service teachers and its impact on their teaching. They go on to say that play, when implemented intentionally, is a powerful pedagogical tool that can be used to support pre-service teacher development. In summary, Husbye's research underscores the importance of incorporating play into teacher education to better prepare preservice teachers for the complexities of real-world teaching. Through a qualitative lens, the study highlights how play can serve as a vital component in developing the adaptive and reflective capabilities of future educators, especially within the context of literacy teaching.

Assessment of the Research

This research study began with a brief overview of the state of research regarding play in education, noting especially the lack of research of the use of play within upper-level educational contexts. Husbye utilized this brief literature review well to both set the stage as to why this particular research is necessary and beneficial as well as to provide the commonly used definitions play used within education. He concludes this section by saying “play, I argue, while serious, encourages preservice teachers to become aware of and negotiate the variety of factors impacting any given teaching experience and the ways that the manipulation of these factors can influence the teaching and learning experience” (p. 3). This sentiment also serves as Husbye’s purpose statement and research question and is referenced throughout the rest of this article. While never explicitly stated in the article as purpose statement and research question, the framing of the above quote and the fact that Husbye continuously refers back to this idea when discussing the actual research, findings, and conclusions and implications makes it clear enough to the reader what the goal of this research is without having to provide explicit line items that eat up precious space in a published article.

The sample taken seems to be one of convivence as it is implied that the researcher is conducting research within classes they are already teaching at the university in question. Given that this is a small-scale, multiple case study qualitative research study focused on exploration of a new concept, this sampling make sense and is appropriate for the research conducted. Additionally, since the participants were working with the researcher over multiple courses and extended period of time, this allowed for development of a strong researcher/participant relationship that can facilitate more accurate data collection and coding. As can be seen, the researcher collected a wide variety of data over the course of the five semesters the study took place and were then coded and analyzed. The article indicates that Transana was used for transcription of any audio/video recordings and that physical artifacts such as instructional plans were uploaded via PDF to support coding across the multiple modalities. The coding took place in two phases. First seeking to identify “moments of change” within the pre-service teachers’ practice. The second phase then “sought to understand the role of play” in supporting these changes. Husbye goes on to say that in this coding and analysis, they were particularly concerned with “durable” changes in the pre-service teachers’ practice. Additionally, to develop triangulation of the coding of the recordings of the rehearsals, Husbye utilized the mid- and end-of-semester interview and instructional reflections as a form of member checking.

With all of this data, Husbye then goes on to present three distinct case studies from the rehearsal data that emphasize three key characteristics of play that Husbye argues are key to durable change in pre-service teacher’s practice. It is interesting to note that these case studies are not all “successes” as in the third case study, the pre-service teacher completed the activity as intended in the rehearsal but in the live classroom felt the pressures of time and reverted to “what they knew” and “how they had been taught.” The following conversation was a great example of the non-durableness of play if a pre-service teacher does not receive the support of cooperating teachers and administration in the school they are placed in. There was a lack of discussion of further details regarding the aggregate coding and analysis of data in favor of presenting more detail and analysis of the three case studies which I think is appropriate given that this article chose to highlight these three case studies from the larger set of data. The presentation of micro-transcripts followed by both student/teacher analysis “in the moment” and additional meta-analysis provided by the researcher provides a well-rounded picture of the case studies and the potential serious play presents to alter a pre-service teacher’s practice in a positive, durable way.

Finally, Husbye concludes with their discussion and explicitly discusses that serious play as used in their research is effective because it helps pre-service teachers develop complexity tolerance which is a key factor in practicing teacher success and longevity. Additionally, Husbye asserts that these rehearsals centered on play allowed pre-service teachers to lose “that fictious control with a lesson plan and were asked to consider how they, as educators, might respond to the children they were about to teach and consider some of the variables that may impact that teaching, spinning it off in a variety of directions” (p. 9). In other words, Husbye is utilizing those practices we are encouraged to use with our K-12 students to make education and learning more authentic with our pre-services teachers with the goal of helping them learn more durable teaching frameworks rather than specific strategies to help them deal with the complex and fluid nature of teaching.

Overall, I felt that the research, its presentation in the article, and additional findings and implications was well done in the small space a published article allows. The tone was formal but approachable and you are left with the impression that the researcher has conducted a solid research study and developed some interesting strategies and ideas surrounding pre-service teacher education that have the potential to be impactful to all teachers. The paper is well-structured and clear. My only quibble is with the very brief discussion of coding and analysis but given that this is a case study and the case studies are well presented with clear analysis from multiple levels, I think that the brevity around the aggregate coding and analysis makes sense. Finally, Husbye’s conclusion and finding is clearly stated near the end of the article and presents a clear path forward for additional research into this topic.
