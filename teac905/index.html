What this course was

A practicum, which in this program means teaching alongside a faculty member and getting credit for the hours. Mine was TEAC 259A — the undergraduate instructional technology course for pre-service elementary teachers — co-taught with my advisor at the time, Dr. Amanda Henson. I was remote for most of the term while I recovered from surgery, so the work was the work that travels: grading, preparing class materials, and holding additional office hours outside class for students who wanted them.

The course runs on micro-credentials. Students earn badges — forty-six of them in the set the course uses, from Apple Teacher and Google Educator through Scratch, Seesaw, green screen, stop-motion, and a Jeopardy game — by building the thing and showing it. What I made for the students myself is small and public: a four-minute introduction video, because most of them would meet me on a screen before they met me in a room, and a deck that opens with the question Who am I? and hides easter eggs for anyone who clicks around. Both went on a page on this site built for the students, and the page kept the chat that built it.

Dissertation corpus

Through line

In the fall of 2022 I sat in Survey of Instructional Technology and uploaded, as week-one evidence, the Apple Teacher and Google Educator certificates I had earned as a classroom teacher. In the fall of 2025 I helped run the undergraduate version of the same course, and both of those certificates are badges in its set. Same subject, other side of the desk, three years apart. The program opened with a survey of instructional technology and, on the teaching side, closed with one.

The students were pre-service teachers. The next summer I built a thirty-item instrument to measure mathematics teacher identity in exactly that population — Subscale 1, previous experiences as a learner — and I wrote the items after a semester of grading their work, not before. The instrument is on the EDPS 870 page; this is where the respondents it imagines were real people with names in a gradebook.

In my first week on the discussion board in 2022 I wrote that I “hope to return around the time I finish my Ph.D.,” and that I did not know whether I would go back to being a K–12 teacher. This was the return. It was remote, it was undergraduates, and it was done while recovering from surgery. Still teaching. Not the way the sentence imagined.
