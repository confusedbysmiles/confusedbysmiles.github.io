What this course was

Eight weeks of seminar with Wayne Babchuk, the second course I took with him, and the one where the methods stopped being a survey and became tools I had to pick up. I critiqued Braun and Clarke’s Thematic Analysis: A Practical Guide as a textbook — what it does well, what it leaves out, and their fifteen-point checklist for whether an analysis has actually been done. In a group with Nathan Speer and Zohreh Tamimdari I presented the first chapter of Saldaña’s Coding Manual, and then narrative inquiry as a methodology: its forms, its sub-approaches, how the stories get analyzed.

The Maxi Project was the YPAR proposal a third time — after EDPS 900K in April and TEAC 880E in May — extended to twenty-five slides and carried into the same term as the pilot study it described, which was by then approved and in the field.

Dissertation corpus

Through line

The two books I stood up and talked about in this course are the two the dissertation’s qualitative strand now cites. The purpose statement says the narrative data will be analyzed with descriptive thematic analysis and pattern coding, and cites Saldaña 2021 for the coding — the same book, the same year, as the critique deck’s reference list. I did not learn these methods for the dissertation. I learned them here, for a different study, and the dissertation inherited them.

Slide 8 of the narrative inquiry presentation lists the forms narrative research can take: biography, autobiography, autoethnography, art-based, life history, life story, lived experience. One word in a list of seven, in a group deck, in October 2024. The dissertation was reframed as an autoethnography in February 2026. I had presented the method as one option among several fifteen months before I noticed it was mine.

And the midterm asked, as its third question, where I stand among the paradigms and what that stance does to my research — the same question EDPS 900K had asked as its first, seven months earlier, with the same instructor. I answered it twice.
