Validity Table: Convergent Design

Section Overview

Module 8, March 2026. The course template names five stages at which quantitative and qualitative strands must be integrated in a convergent design — design, sampling, data collection, analysis, interpretation — and the threat to validity that lives at each. The left column of each entry is what the design calls for, from the template. The right column is my plan, written for a study in which the researcher and the sole participant are the same person, which changes what several of the threats mean.

Addressing Threats to Validity in Convergent Designs

Integration During Design

Threat Choosing a mixed methods core design that does not align with the research questions/aims.

What a convergent design calls for

Selecting a convergent design when research questions/aims call for quantitative and qualitative procedures that address similar or parallel concepts.

My plan

Given that this is an auto-ethnography, I am both researcher and participant, so I don’t need the quantitative analysis to identify qualitative participants or inform my interview protocol. Additionally, the quantitative and qualitative strands are focused on the same topics and will be woven together to strengthen the research as a whole.

Integration During Sampling (sample relationship, alignment, and sample size)

Threat The relationship between the quantitative and qualitative samples does not align with the study’s research questions/aims and the sample sizes do not align with their respective tradition’s guidelines.

What a convergent design calls for

Selecting an appropriate sample relationship between the quantitative and qualitative samples and using respective sample size guidelines for each approach, while also considering necessary adjustments based on the research questions/aims.

My plan

Given that this is an auto-ethnography, I am both researcher and participant. This is appropriate for both the qualitative and quantitative strands and align with the research questions. This does by definition also constrain sample size for both strands, which is still appropriate for the quantitative strand as the goal is to use quantitative tools and reasoning to analyze artifacts generated in the qualitative auto-ethnography to provide additional context and support while also generating pathways for future, extending research.

Integration During Data Collection

Threat Failure to appropriately select data collection methods that do not align with the research questions or are not appropriate for the sample(s).

What a convergent design calls for

When collecting data simultaneously for the quantitative and qualitative components, especially from the same sample, ensure the data collection methods do not overburden the participants involved in both components of the study.

My plan

The qualitative and quantitative research questions provide data about my experiences with technology, mathematics, and teaching in a variety of ways and will be coded for themes. Simultaneously, the quantitative data will help me to identify trends in that data which will help develop my qualitative research, and this will continue to spiral and converge as appropriate to answer my MMR research question, which is--to what extent do documented patterns in my technology experiences complement and expand upon narrative accounts of critical moments in my development as an educator focused on equity and access in mathematics?

As this is an auto-ethnography and I am the only participant, and simultaneously the researcher, I plan to break data collection into smaller chunks to allow for more manageable pieces.

Integration During Analysis

Threat Analytic techniques are not appropriate or do not align with the respective quantitative and qualitative designs and research questions/aims.

What a convergent design calls for

Selecting analytic techniques that align with the respective quantitative and qualitative designs and will yield quantitative results and qualitative findings that can be merged to form a more complete picture of the study topic.

Selecting data transformation procedures (if applicable) that are most appropriate for quantitizing and/or qualitizing data that will yield results that can be merged to form a more complete picture of the study topic.

My plan

The analysis of my data will answer my research questions due to the nature and quality of the data collected, as discussed above. Additionally, data will be presented in a joint display for readability and transparency. Quantitative summary descriptive statistics will also be utilized to help connect and extend qualitative findings.

Integration During Interpretation

Threat Findings from the quantitative and qualitative phases are not adequately integrated to generate sound meta-inferences. Failure to adequately address divergent findings.

What a convergent design calls for

Selecting integration methods such as joint displays or integrative narratives to merge the independently collected and analyzed quantitative and qualitative data to directly compare findings from both phases.

Addressing divergent findings by providing possible explanation for the divergence.

My plan

My research is an auto-ethnography in both design and method; and this, coupled with my joint display will allow for a thorough, nuanced interpretation of my collected data. Because I am both participant and researcher, I am guaranteed to find themes and trends that do not align with my preconceived notions of myself and my identity as teacher. These will be handled with reflection and additional cycles of qualitative and quantitative reflection, analysis, and integration of the new data.

Stage and threat language follows the course template (after Creswell & Plano Clark, 2018). The plans are mine as submitted; the study’s quantitative strand has since been narrowed to summary statistics on my own artifact data.
